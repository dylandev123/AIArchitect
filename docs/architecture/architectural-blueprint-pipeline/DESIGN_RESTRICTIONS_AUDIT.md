# Architectural intelligence and design restrictions audit

21 October 2026, Saint Lucia. Offline source audit, separately authored design comparisons, mocked production-route verification and production-workspace captures. No Terra/OpenAI requests, paid generations, model/flag/env-file changes, commits, pushes, deployments, V2 changes or advanced-terrain changes.

The immediate bottleneck in the two recovered estates is underdeveloped architectural authorship, amplified by a technically demanding input contract and real capability ceilings. The production builder did not turn intricate plans into rectangles: both original responses explicitly chose rectangular buildings. This change makes architectural composition the first instruction, removes avoidable initial-detail obligations, and corrects a footprint-dependent drop-off restriction. It does **not** establish that Terra will naturally produce better houses. That requires a later controlled author evaluation.

## How the actual authoring process works

The flagged route obtains `v3ArchitecturalBlueprintPrompt()` and sends it as `system` with the original brief and `Output.json`. The dedicated prompt includes the executable **input** schema. It does not send the regular detailed-scene authoring prompt or any of the offline redesigns. Default house model selection remains the existing Terra policy in `model.ts`, with the existing environment override; this audit does not change it.

The previous source already asked for estate planning, functional rooms, massing and exterior work. Merely adding another request to “be creative” would duplicate that. Its weaknesses were prioritization, repeated technical detail, “simple” phrasing, expensive required empty ledgers and no external quality judgment. The revised prompt begins with composition, chooses style from the actual brief, makes footprint/room/circulation decisions dependent, and asks for a final comparison between the promised experience and executable geometry. Climate, orientation, views, privacy, arrival, courtyards/wings, outdoor rooms, roof character, facade depth and applied materials are explicit considerations. They remain author decisions, not new compulsory schema fields.

This remains one buffered response containing plan followed by exterior. Local consumers enforce the plan/massing/exterior order, but there is no live human checkpoint or second AI call between them, and no direct access to the model's reasoning. Geometry can be coherent and still mediocre. Neither validation nor the prompt can certify taste.

### Why the recovered houses were basic

Both original documents choose two rectangular buildings with unchanged outer envelopes through their storeys. The first uses a 24×18m two-storey main house and an 18×12m guest building; the second uses the same main envelope and a 12×8m guest pavilion. The first has no authored assemblies beyond derived entrance canopies; the second likewise has no explicit facade assemblies. Their forms are declared rectangular in their own ledgers. The compiler substantially follows those decisions.

The first places a broad north terrace and a pool with a **4m envelope gap**, and a rim datum at 1.5m versus paving at .18m (**1.32m difference**). The second has a **2m envelope gap** and **1.22m rim difference**. These are authored relationships, not hidden renderer substitutions. Room footprints often resemble corner-based tiling but are interpreted as centres; many original zones conflict. Several interior partitions have no valid shared boundary. The second entry conflicts with its service rhythm. Fixing one entry/window defect does not develop the silhouette, shade or estate composition.

The available evidence supports a combination of underdeveloped initial design and contract/capability pressure. It cannot identify exactly which instruction caused the historical model choice. Rectangles can be excellent; here their proportions, openings, shallow shade, outdoor relationships and landscape do not sufficiently develop the ambitious luxury prose. Fixed material/landscape/pool policies and aerial presentation further lower the ceiling.

## What changed and who owns each decision

The revised author instruction shrinks from 9,130 to 6,309 characters (about 31%); prompt plus schema from 36,381 to 33,755 (about 7%). The schema gains explicit room/stair centre descriptions and true optional supporting collections. These are character counts, not tokenizer measurements or historical billing figures.

| Terra decides once | V3 derives deterministically |
| --- | --- |
| Concept, style and estate hierarchy, required program/constraints | Semantic reference indices and consistency diagnostics |
| Site dimensions/access, world building/feature placements and exact supported outlines | Runtime transforms, outline envelopes, wall/floor construction and curve tessellation |
| Main room zones, storey heights, entrance wall addresses, stair/lift reservation/type/direction | Storey elevations, total height, usable flights/landings and entrance world contacts |
| Outdoor geometry and coordinated vertical datums; dry contacts, route width/use/bends | Contact world coordinates, canonical route ribbon and selection bounds |
| Reserved roof identity and compatible roof family/pitch/overhang/thickness | Host roof origin/yaw/footprint/bearing datum and supported roof construction |
| Glazing, shade/assembly intent, applied materials; detailed partitions/doors only when needed | Host-relative facade construction and shared room boundaries/cutouts |

Technical roof thickness, span fractions, stair reservations and explicit part decomposition remain author inputs because the current builders do not infer their intended architecture. This change does not pretend to implement a concept-only generator. Stable roof/entrance identities are still necessary references; they are not duplicate geometry. Existing complete blueprints and saved scenes keep their prior contract and loading behavior; no migration was added.

## Real generation path and reliability

With `AI_V3_ARCHITECTURAL_BLUEPRINT=1`, only a new flat-site generation enters `generateArchitecturalBlueprintV3`; it obtains the revised prompt and calls `generateV3FromArchitecturalBlueprint` with `requirePlanFirst:true`. Legacy complete-blueprint input is refused for new authoring, while offline recovery remains supported. Strict exterior schemas refuse a second independently authored scene or plan override.

Sequence: `prepareV3ArchitecturalPlan` (no detailed compiler) → `constructV3PlanMassing` (existing compiler) → `elaborateV3ArchitecturalPlan` (ID-bound decisions) → existing blueprint approval/construction/revalidation/receipts → exact plan comparison. Only ordinary V3 semantic plan fields are saved; no second mutable plan copy. The separate massing projection is presentation data. A single response still contains both initial plan and exterior choices; there is no live design feedback between these stages.

The actual SDK/route tests inspect the transmitted system schema, return raw JSON omitting new optional ledgers/details, and verify persisted plan-first provenance. They also exercise the original recovered drafts, curved/courtyard/classical compatibility, rejection of scene drift/invalid geometry, sole-stair failures, output truncation, zero SDK retries and at most one diagnosed stair/waypoint repair. Direct vehicle-entry clearance remains tested separately from a drop-off. Authored reservations still produce the same conflicts; only omission of an empty ledger has changed.

Token limit remains 10,000 output tokens per response. Maximum author attempts remain two: one initial response and at most one objective repair, no transport retry. Optional detail and shorter instructions may reduce output pressure; rich estate designs can still exhaust the cap and are still rejected without accepting truncated JSON. The schema is still the largest input component. Character reduction is measured; reliability/latency/billing gains and future natural-author output quality are unmeasured. No actual model evaluation or token usage was purchased.

## Validation and evaluation decision

Focused checks passed 64 tests across four files. The full suite passed **2,360 tests**, with **3 skipped**, **0 failed**, across 195 files. TypeScript, ESLint, the optimized production build and whitespace checks passed. See [checks.json](design-restrictions/checks.json) for commands, exits and counts. Required checks include focused regressions, full Vitest suite, TypeScript, ESLint, optimized production build, whitespace and original fixture preservation. The unrestricted full-suite run alongside compilation hit 43 timeouts; a full rerun with two workers passed without changing assertions or timeouts. The first sandbox build failed fetching existing Google Fonts; a network-enabled build is recorded separately. No font or renderer code was changed to disguise that environment failure.

**GO for one controlled architectural-plan evaluation after separate authorization to spend.** The authoring path, geometry authority and bounded-call behavior are testable offline and remain intact. This is **NO-GO for claiming proven natural Terra quality, broad rollout, complete landscape/interior construction or automatic luxury-brief satisfaction**.

The later evaluation should use one open architectural brief without a supplied template, record the exact prompt/schema hash, raw first response, finish reason, real usage and any bounded repair; score the first 2D drawing before examining materials. Review arrival, privacy/circulation, footprint proportions, meaningful courtyard/outdoor relationships, climate response and correspondence between concept and geometry. Compare the same production views and verify exact plan/massing/exterior authority. An honest restrained plan can pass; forced complexity cannot. This audit does not execute or authorize that provider request.
