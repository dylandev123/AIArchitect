# First live coordinated-assembly test — UNAPPROVED

**Verdict: assembly adoption succeeded; the requested architectural-quality success was not achieved.** Terra independently chose both new operations. The result has better coordinated pool construction and a clearer main/secondary roof hierarchy, but still reads as three simple detached rectangular blocks. It is not convincingly a sophisticated tropical luxury residence.

## Execution and preflight

Exactly one paid OpenAI request reached `gpt-5.6-terra`; zero retries, repair calls, or additional AI calls. The unchanged homepage endpoint `POST /api/ai/scene` was invoked by a local harness, rather than by clicking the browser Generate button. Source tracing confirms the homepage routes through direct-scene project creation and OperationStudio to this endpoint. The exact serialized provider request confirms both assembly schemas and usage instructions reached Terra.

41 offline tests passed across assembly integration, provider serialization, and knowledge integration. They cover child editing/history, whole-wing transforms, roof and shared-junction rejection, rotated envelopes, pool basin boundaries and dry circulation, and the unified knowledge path. The documented parameters match the executor: single-storey wings with solid rear/sides and front bays; pool placement anchored at the near dry deck rather than basin centre. They do not create interiors, roof unions or passages automatically.

The first sandbox preflight failed at knowledge-database DNS before any OpenAI request. The subsequent network-enabled execution retrieved the real connected knowledge and made the sole paid call. The endpoint's pre-provider error incorrectly reported `providerCalls:1`; the transport counter establishes zero for that preflight and one for the completed generation.

Actual provider usage: **10,018 input + 7,809 output = 17,827 tokens**, including 4,942 reasoning tokens; cached input reported as zero. See `usage.json` and the raw provider response. No price estimate is substituted for provider usage.

## Requested findings

1. **New operations invoked:** three `residential-wing` assemblies and one `rectangular-pool` assembly, chosen by Terra from the ordinary instructions and user brief. No developer-authored house operations were supplied.
2. **Constructed through assemblies:** `main-living` (18 × 9 m, 3.6 m enclosure, gable roof); `west-suite` and `east-suite` (each 8 × 7 m, lower flat roofs); `sea-pool` (8 × 10 m water, 1.45 m basin depth). Assemblies expanded into slabs, walls, front-bay openings, glazing/entrances, reveals and roofs, and pool floor, perimeter walls, water, coping and surrounding dry deck. They initially created **62 of 73 elements (~85%)**. The audit's 63 assembly-associated elements also includes the separately added arrival door attached into the main assembly.
3. **Coordination retained:** zero construction relationship errors; stable parent/child IDs; supported scene serialization. Browser selection, material change, Undo, Redo and reload passed on `main-living-roof`. Whole-wing move/rotation/scale was verified offline, not applied to this retained generated scene. Parent relationships coordinate children; independently placed terrace/gallery/approach parts do not automatically follow a later wing move.
4. **Roof and pool conflicts:** zero pairwise roof-plan-envelope overlaps and zero plates crossing the open water plane. Focused pool construction checks found no basin-wall gaps or shallow-basin warnings. Unsafe junction rejection passed offline, but this single output does not prove the guards prevented a conflict Terra would otherwise have created. Site excavation and hydraulics remain absent. Pool dry deck top is Y=.30 versus the approach/terrace Y=.20: a 10 cm transition remains. The claimed .5 m terrace clearance is contradicted by .25 m plan overlap with the near pool deck; it is a dry-deck overlap, not water occlusion.
5. **Primitive reliance:** the operation list is 4 assemble, 11 add and 1 replace. Primitives numerically dominate operation count, but assemblies do most actual construction. Primitives provide gallery, terrace, entry portal/canopy, door, approach, sofas and one prebuilt curved pavilion. This is meaningful assembly adoption.
6. **Composition and visual quality:** a higher gabled communal hall, lower side suites, a glazed sea-facing frontage, stone deck and offset pool produce readable hierarchy. Roofs are separated and do not form oversized repetitive overlaps. However, the symmetrical detached boxes, long blank arrival wall and tiny canopy are plain; zero-overhang assembly roofs provide weak tropical shading. The curved lounge is an existing reusable visual feature, rather than a newly developed architectural solution. No actual sea or coastal landscape is rendered.
7. **Remaining defects:** no modeled door from the main living hall onto its terrace (all five sea-front inserts are glazing); no bathrooms or subdivided suites; no equipped kitchen/service programme; gallery only 1 m deep, largely uncovered despite the protected-gallery narrative, and ends at the suite-door centre lines rather than spanning their full thresholds. Five declared spatial relationships fail the heuristic audit; some are bounding-envelope/tolerance warnings, not proof of unusability. The curved pavilion exceeds the terrace's conservative envelope and is a single selectable, version-pinned component: its internal roof/furniture are **not individually editable**, so the full component-level editability requirement is unmet. Furniture floor contact and gable-end weather enclosure also require review. This is an exterior concept, not a complete functioning residence.
8. **Better than previous Terra houses:** compared with the retained `distinct-houses/A` tropical example using the same camera positions, the pool basin/deck is more complete and wings have explicit construction. Overall visual improvement is **mixed and insufficient**: the earlier design had more vertical layering; the new one is a simpler single-level three-block composition. No claim that this is categorically better than all prior Terra houses is warranted. The user-defined success condition therefore fails despite successful assembly use.
