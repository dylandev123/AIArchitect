# Architectural freedom in the existing direct-scene engine

Implemented offline, 29 October 2026. **Normal Generate can now author independently editable polygon and arc extrusions. No live Terra generation was made: autonomous design quality remains unproven.** This expands the existing element/operation contract and `OperationScene`, not a second construction engine. Existing uncommitted work was preserved.

1. **Which architectural restrictions were removed?** Removed the provider-context Solana layout description (salon, subordinate dining pavilion, recessed suite and stone arrival portal), the suggested estate organization of wings/pavilions/courts, and the requirement to consider at least two concepts. Replaced these with brief-led design authority, without a replacement menu. Removed default retrieval boosts for `estate-composition`, `compact-family`, `base-middle-top`, `private-bedroom-wing`, `window-rhythm` and `layered-roofline`. These records remain available when relevant; no knowledge records were deleted or rewritten. Removed prescribed stone entrance composition and the instruction to recess upper storeys. Updated capability claims that previously restricted curved roofs and nonrectangular solids to the pinned pavilion. Exterior-first scope, accommodation capacity, property-value/budget distinction, climate/site context and user exclusions remain.

2. **Which prompts/examples biased Terra?** `ARCHITECTURAL_CONCEPT_GUIDANCE` was appended by `architecturalRequestContext` to actual Generate knowledge context, so its Solana description and estate organization advice reached the provider. `SCENE_SYSTEM_PROMPT` included prescribed entrance parts and rectangular-only capability claims. Retrieval gave specific composition and facade records unconditional residential boosts. The guide, system prompt, planning instructions, assembly documentation, derived capability reference, knowledge retrieval, property-value guidance, reviewed-lesson context, strict schema adapter and normal route were inspected. Knowledge and reviewed lessons remain explicitly optional contextual evidence; their historical suggestions cannot override the brief or construction contract. Current source knowledge records are preserved, including the existing property-value additions; this work does not claim new knowledge ingestion or comprehensive semantic brief extraction.

3. **Which assembly assumptions were restrictive?** `residential-wing` still intentionally constructs a rectangular single-storey shell with three solid elevations and front bays. Its roof builders remain flat/gable/hip. Those are genuine helper semantics, so silently changing them would break old projects. The helper is now explicitly optional throughout the instructions; direct profile parts bypass its footprint, elevation and facade assumptions. Unequal `bayWidths`, configurable heights, yaw, entry-bay selection, separate facade infill and individual child edits were already present in the working tree and are not claimed as new here. Nonuniform hierarchy scaling with rotated descendants can require shear; its existing protection remains. Programme reference limits were widened to 128 enclosure IDs and 64 floor/roof/access IDs per group so faceted construction is not forced into a few box references. The 400-element/800-operation limits remain.

4. **What geometry is genuinely supported?** An optional `geometry` on existing plate/wall/roof/volume/glazing/entrance elements supports:

   - Simple convex or concave polygon extrusions with up to eight disjoint interior holes, along X, Y or Z.
   - Parameterized annular circular/elliptical arcs and rings, with independently editable start, sweep, inner ratio and tessellation.
   - Irregular floor/roof/terrace footprints, faceted enclosures, curved walls and glazing, polygon openings, extruded portal sections, sloped members, folded sections and barrel-like canopies.

   Polygon coordinates span normalized `[-0.5,0.5]` bounds; the existing `size` sets metres. Arc bounds are normalized after sampling, so partial-arc `at` is its bounding-box centre, not the generating ellipse centre. Profiles remain closed solids with real empty cutouts. Curves are polygonal approximations. Limits: 128 vertices per ring, 256 total, 4–64 arc segments, bounded finite coordinates and dimensions. Duplicate edges, zero areas, self-crossings, intersecting/outside/touching/nested holes, incompatible legacy opening/roof parameters, invalid IDs and attachment cycles fail. Triangle-contact work has a two-million-comparison guard. No executable geometry input is accepted.

5. **Can Terra create nonrectangular and curved architecture?** It has the exposed schema and executor to do so. Actual offline route/SDK transport replays of all three studies preserve their profiles, coordinates, materials and IDs exactly, including strict nullable fields. Nothing simplifies them into boxes. This proves availability and execution, not autonomous model use. The studies are developer-authored feasibility constructions, not fixed Generate templates, finished residences or evidence of architectural sophistication from Terra. They are imported only by offline tests/scripts; Generate does not receive them.

6. **Are roofs more varied?** Existing flat/gable/hip builders remain compatible. Plan extrusions support irregular polygon roofs and courtyard/skylight cutouts. Vertical section extrusions support butterfly, asymmetric and folded profiles; arc sections support sampled barrel roofs/canopies. Individual sections can be positioned and yawed independently. Roof thickness, overhangs and voids are authored in the profile; separate pieces can have different finishes. Junctions are manually coordinated, not automatically unioned. A general compound roof or arbitrary double-curved surface is not implemented.

7. **Is the geometry independently editable?** Yes, per element: stable ID, profile/arc parameters and holes, dimensions, position, yaw, material and optional hex color survive JSON save/reload and project-store Undo/Redo. Ancestor-local positions/yaw still inherit recursively. The existing studio now exposes an explicit-apply profile JSON editor, rotation and color alongside its position, dimension and finish controls. Profile roof elements do not show the incompatible legacy roofForm selector. This is parameter/vertex editing, not a visual vertex gizmo or arbitrary XYZ rotation. Multiple materials use separate editable elements; profile faces do not have individually assigned materials. Profile meshes use standard colored PBR materials, without the legacy box finish textures or automatic glazing mullions.

   The actual browser studio test imports the flowing study, changes the roof's curve parameters, moves/resizes/rotates/recolors it, exports JSON, compares local storage, reloads and verifies the edited controls. All 39 sibling elements remain byte-equivalent as parsed JSON. See [studio evidence](studio.browser-result.json) and [edited scene](studio-edited.json). Automated tests separately exercise real project-store Undo/Redo and nested rotated attachments.

8. **Are these exposed to normal Generate?** Yes. `elementSchema` feeds `operationSchema`, the derived capability reference and actual strict provider response schema. The existing strict adapter now handles nested geometry union branches and optional null properties. The normal route and real OpenAI SDK are tested with mocked transport; zero requests reach a model. Execution remains `stageSceneOperations` / `applySceneOperations`, and rendering remains `OperationScene`. Historical scenes with no geometry field retain their previous builders and format. No model, retry, timeout, token cap, renderer replacement or second generation pipeline was added.

9. **What limitations remain, and why?** Extrusion is the reusable implemented subset; arbitrary indexed meshes, CSG/booleans, swept splines/NURBS, non-extrudable sculptural forms and double-curved surfaces remain unsupported. Element transforms retain yaw rather than arbitrary pitch/roll; sloped sections can instead be authored through the profile. Water remains rectangular; hydraulic systems, arbitrary pool shells, terrain-aware Generate, automatic layout/route planning and structural/code certification remain outside this work.

   Existing rectangle checks are retained for historical geometry. New profiles use rendered triangles for support/contact and water-obstruction checks, and actual horizontal cap triangles for full straight-railing support, including holes. Occupied-volume roof checks use actual profile solids when the floor is a horizontal plan extrusion. Other vertical-section floors, complete overhead unions, full passage/headroom and complex spatial-intent proofs remain explicitly unverified. Rectangular roof/perimeter/area evidence cannot certify profile construction; review reports this rather than calling bounds filled architecture. Custom profile supports require explicit placement: `place-on` deliberately refuses to infer a horizontal surface from their bounds. No existing safety check was globally disabled.

10. **Smallest future model test?** One normal Generate using a real irregular/curved exterior brief, without the studies or a preset composition in its context. Retain the complete request, response and construction diagnostics; check all brief requirements and inspect arrival, aerial and poolside views plus component edits/save. That would test whether Terra actually selects and coordinates the new vocabulary. It would not establish reliability, visual quality across price bands or engineering correctness. No live call was made or scheduled.

**Verification:** 239 tests pass across 40 files; typecheck and focused ESLint pass. Tests include invalid geometry, normalized bounds and oriented solid volume in all three extrusion axes, courtyard support/water/roof holes, true roof intrusion rejection, railing coverage, independent curve edits, nested transforms, strict Generate transport, compatibility, persistence and real history. A 400-profile scene is constructed and reloaded under the existing resource cap. Local study construction took roughly 20–36 ms in the recorded run, not a universal performance guarantee. Nine captures verify exact IDs/local transforms, selected camera poses, actual profile triangle counts, no browser errors and no WebGL context loss. All API and non-loopback requests were blocked. Browser evidence is a local feasibility check, not a comprehensive GPU benchmark.
